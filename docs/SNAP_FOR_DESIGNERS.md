# Snap: Build Scenes, Explore Ideas

Snap is a visual scene-building tool for arranging modular 3D pieces into environments, set concepts, and gameplay spaces. It is made for the early, creative stage of world building: the moment when you want to try an idea quickly, move things around, and see the composition take shape.

## Start With a Mood, Not a Blueprint

Choose a library, browse its pieces, and place them directly into the scene. A medieval space can begin with a floor, a wall, and a roof; then grow through furniture, lighting, props, and small storytelling details. You do not need to prepare every object before you begin. Snap is designed for building by adjustment.

Use the search field and category filters to find what you need. Asset names and tags help surface related pieces, whether you are looking for a table, a chest, a banner, or a roof part.

## Shape the Space

Snap helps you keep layouts tidy while you experiment. Grid snapping makes modular parts line up; rotation controls let you turn pieces into place; and height tools help position objects above the ground. You can place a single asset, duplicate it, or assemble several pieces into a group.

Groups are especially useful for reusable ideas: a market stall, a reading corner, a small house façade, or a complete room. Move or organize the group as one, then keep refining its contents whenever you need to.

## Work in Layers

The Scene Tree gives you a clear view of everything in the current scene. Rename objects and groups so the layout stays understandable, hide parts temporarily to focus on an area, and lock finished elements before working nearby. This makes it easier to explore alternatives without losing a good version of the scene.

## Keep the Good Ideas

Save work in the browser while you are developing a scene. When you want to continue elsewhere or share the editable layout, export a Snap JSON scene file. You can also export the visible scene as GLB, or as a zipped GLTF package, for use in other 3D tools and pipelines.

## A Practical Creative Loop

1. Pick a library and search for a starting piece.
2. Block out the largest forms first: floors, walls, roofs, or platforms.
3. Add furniture, props, and decorations to establish purpose and mood.
4. Group sections that belong together and use the Scene Tree to keep them organized.
5. Save a version when the scene reaches a useful milestone.
6. Export when the idea is ready to move into the next stage of production.

Snap is most effective when it stays playful. Build a quick version, change your mind, make a second version, and let the scene tell you what it needs.
